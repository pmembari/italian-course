# Lezione 3 — Visual exercises, speaking and mixed review

[← Lesson & conversations](lesson.md) · [Grammar](grammar.md) · [Vocabulary](vocabulary.md) · **Exercises**

> This workbook adds **original practice** aligned with *Nuovo Espresso 1* pp. **33–46**; page **47** opens the next lesson. The original publisher's listening checks depend on authorized audio/video and are **not copied or marked complete** here.

## How to practise actively

1. **Cover** the Italian answers first.
2. Say a complete sentence **aloud** before opening each answer.
3. For each error, repeat a corrected sentence with a different food or number.
4. Mix vocabulary from **Lessons 1–2** (greetings, polite `Lei`) into Lesson 3 ordering situations.
5. Use the illustrations to recall words before you read their labels.

## Part G — Short reading: a fictional menu

> **Trattoria dei Limoni — Menù di oggi**  
> **Antipasti:** bruschette, insalata.  
> **Primi:** risotto alle verdure, spaghetti al pomodoro.  
> **Secondi:** pesce alla griglia, pollo con patate.  
> **Dolci:** gelato, frutta fresca.  
> **Bevande:** acqua naturale, spremuta, tè.

**61.** Which category includes risotto?  
**62.** What could a guest who doesn't eat meat or fish choose from the first courses?  
**63.** What could they choose for dessert?

<details><summary>Reading answers</summary>

61. **Primi**. 62. **Risotto alle verdure** or **spaghetti al pomodoro**. 63. **Gelato** or **frutta fresca**. Note that ingredients, allergies and dietary requirements require separate verification; a menu category alone doesn't guarantee suitability.

</details>

## Part I — Sentence ladders and memory transfer

**67.** See a fork → say noun + article → one sentence → polite request.

<details><summary>Model chain</summary>

`la forchetta` → `Manca una forchetta.` → `Mi porta una forchetta, per favore?`

</details>

**68.** Say the English *“I'd like a tea”*, then create the correct Italian sentence. Explain why `voglio` changes the tone.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

`Vorrei un tè.` `Voglio` directly says “I want”; `vorrei` is a polite “I would like” request.

</details>

**69.** Explain why the English *“the pizzas”* requires changes to two words in Italian.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

`la pizza → le pizze`. Italian changes **the article** and the **noun ending**; English *the* does not change.

</details>

**70.** Explain the difference between `buono` and `bene` and give one example of each.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`La pasta è buona` (adjective for a thing); `Qui si mangia bene` (adverb for an action).

</details>

## Part K — Shadowing and spaced-recall challenge

**75.** Read the [original café dialogue](vocabulary.md#shadowing-and-speaking-ladder) slowly, echo it, then hide it and act out both roles.

**76.** Switch the drink, snack and politeness formula. Repeat without reading. With an authorized Italian recording, practise genuine audio shadowing, imitating stress and rhythm; without audio, this is **read-aloud/repetition**, not true shadowing.

**77.** Review by mixing **Lesson 1** greetings, **Lesson 2** language/job introductions and **Lesson 3** restaurant requests in a single fictional 8-line conversation.

## Review checklist

- [ ] I can order a drink and meal with `vorrei` and `prendo`.
- [ ] I can express food preferences with `preferisco`.
- [ ] I can identify menu courses, snacks and table items from pictures.
- [ ] I can ask for missing items and the bill politely.
- [ ] I can make noun/article plurals and distinguish `bene` from `buono`.
- [ ] I can read a restaurant ad and arrange a reservation.
- [ ] I have practised the publisher's exercises **only where the media was accessible**.
- [ ] I can create fresh sentences without mentally translating every English word.

The tasks measure personal practice only; no private attempts, scores or progress are stored in this public repository.
