# Lesson 8 — Grammar, comparisons and memory

[← Lesson](lesson.md) · **Grammar** · [Vocabulary](vocabulary.md) · [Exercises](exercise.md)

**Source:** *Nuovo Espresso 1*, pp. **105–113**, especially **Comunicazione e grammatica p. 113**.

## 1. Quantities and containers

| Quantifier | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **un etto** | `un etto di prosciutto` | 100 grams of ham |
| **due etti** | `due etti di parmigiano` | 200 grams of parmesan |
| **mezzo chilo** | `mezzo chilo di pomodori` | half a kilogram of tomatoes |
| **un chilo** | `un chilo di patate` | one kilogram of potatoes |
| **un litro** | `un litro di latte` | one litre of milk |
| **un vasetto** | `un vasetto di maionese` | a small jar of mayonnaise |
| **una confezione** | `una confezione di biscotti` | a packet of biscuits |

**Visual recall:** picture a deli scale displaying **100 g** when you hear **un etto**. English uses *of* in these phrases; Italian normally uses **di**.

## 2. Quantity requests: `quanto / quanta / quanti / quante`

- `**Quanto** prosciutto vuole?` — How much ham?
- `**Quanta** mozzarella vuole?` — How much mozzarella?
- `**Quanti** panini vuole?` — How many sandwiches?
- `**Quante** olive vuole?` — How many olives?

**Memory:** match the **noun**, not the purchaser. English has much/many; Italian also marks gender.

## 3. Partitive expressions: `del / dello / della / dell' / dei / degli / delle`

- `Vorrei **del** pane.` — I'd like some bread.
- `Compro **della** carne.` — I buy some meat.
- `Prendo **dell'**olio.` — I take some oil.
- `Ho comprato **delle** mele.` — I bought some apples.

In some contexts Italian can use no partitive; don't force *some* into every translation. The article form depends on the following noun.

## 4. Direct-object pronouns: `lo, la, li, le`

| Noun | Pronoun | New sentence |
|---|---|---|
| `il prosciutto` | **lo** | `Lo compro.` |
| `la mozzarella` | **la** | `La prendo.` |
| `i biscotti` | **li** | `Li compro.` |
| `le olive` | **le** | `Le prendo.` |

**Mnemonic:** **lo = one masculine**, **la = one feminine**, **li = many masculine**, **le = many feminine**.

**English bridge:** English *it/them* does not show grammatical gender. Italian object pronouns appear **before** a conjugated verb in the basic models: `La compro`, not `Compro la` for a stand-alone pronoun.

## 5. `ne` for quantities

- `Quante olive vuole?` — `**Ne** vorrei **due etti**.`
- `Vuole del formaggio?` — `Sì, **ne** prendo un po'.`
- `Ha delle mele?` — `Sì, **ne** ho cinque.`

**Memory:** `ne` points to **some of that quantity**. English often leaves this implicit: "I'll take five" versus Italian `Ne prendo cinque`. `Ne` does **not** replace every instance of English *it*; distinguish **lo** from **ne**.

## 6. Shopping structures and polite orders

- `Vorrei due etti di prosciutto, per favore.`
- `Lo vuole fresco o stagionato?`
- `Me ne dà un po'?` — Could you give me some?
- `Nient'altro, grazie.` — Nothing else, thank you.
- `Quant'è?` — How much is it?
- `Ecco il resto.` — Here's the change.

The forms `vorrei` and polite `mi dà / me ne dà` are useful chunks; don't assume **dà** means English *do*.

## 7. Impersonal `si + verb`

- `In Italia **si mangia** spesso la pasta.` — People often eat pasta in Italy.
- `In questo negozio **si compra** il pane.` — Bread is bought in this shop.
- `Qui **si vendono** prodotti tipici.` — Typical products are sold here.

For the impersonal construction with an intransitive or objectless verb, the verb is normally third-person singular (`si mangia`). With a plural noun functioning as the subject of a **si passivante** construction, a plural verb may occur (`si vendono prodotti`). **Don't treat all si sentences as an unconditional singular rule.**

## 8. Recipe sequencing

| Drawing cue | Italian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 🔪 | **tagliare** | Prima si taglia la cipolla. |
| 🥣 | **aggiungere** | Poi si aggiunge il sale. |
| 🫗 | **versare** | Si versa l'olio. |
| 🥄 | **mescolare** | Si mescolano gli ingredienti. |
| 🍲 | **cuocere** | Si cuoce per dieci minuti. |
