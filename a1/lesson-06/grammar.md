# Lezione 6 — Grammar: describing towns and giving directions

> **Book:** opening overview p. 75; **Comunicazione e grammatica p. 85**; examples and pictures pp. 76–84. These grammar explanations, memory diagrams in words, and examples are original.

## 1. `c'è` / `ci sono` and the plural partitive

| One place/object | Several objects |
|---|---|
| **C'è un museo.** — There is a museum. | **Ci sono dei musei.** — There are some museums. |
| **C'è una chiesa.** — There is a church. | **Ci sono delle chiese.** — There are some churches. |
| **C'è un albergo.** | **Ci sono degli alberghi.** |

The plural partitive `dei / degli / delle` is formed historically from **di + definite article**. Its everyday use here is similar to English *some*. **No need to translate “some” in every English sentence**: `Ci sono musei` is also possible.

| Noun | Definite plural | Partitive plural |
|---|---|---|
| palazzi | i palazzi | **dei** palazzi |
| musei | i musei | **dei** musei |
| alberghi | gli alberghi | **degli** alberghi |
| chiese | le chiese | **delle** chiese |

**Memory:** three signs over the nouns: **I → DEI**, **GLI → DEGLI**, **LE → DELLE**.

## 2. Adjectives agree with nouns in gender and number

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | un museo **famoso** | dei musei **famosi** |
| Feminine | una chiesa **famosa** | delle chiese **famose** |
| Masculine -e | un museo **interessante** | dei musei **interessanti** |
| Feminine -e | una mostra **interessante** | delle mostre **interessanti** |

**English bridge:** English *famous* never changes, but Italian `famoso/a/i/e` agrees. Learn **noun + adjective as a pair**.

### Adjectives in -co / -ca (source p. 85)

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| palazzo **antico** | palazzi **antichi** |
| chiesa **antica** | chiese **antiche** |
| ristorante **tipico** | ristoranti **tipici** |
| trattoria **tipica** | trattorie **tipiche** |

**Watch out:** `-co` masculine plurals are not universally `-chi`: `tipico → tipici` but `antico → antichi`. Memorize **these source pairs**, not an invented universal rule.

## 3. `ci` with `andare` means “there”

- `Vai spesso a Padova?` — **`Sì, ci vado spesso.`**
- `Andate in centro?` — **`Sì, ci andiamo.`**

**English bridge:** The word `ci` here points back to a **place mentioned before**, close to English *there*. Elsewhere `ci` has other uses; don't think it always means “we” or “there”.

## 4. `dovere` and `sapere` (source p. 79)

| Person | dovere — must/have to | sapere — know |
|---|---|---|
| io | **devo** | **so** |
| tu | **devi** | **sai** |
| lui / lei / Lei | **deve** | **sa** |
| noi | **dobbiamo** | **sappiamo** |
| voi | **dovete** | **sapete** |
| loro | **devono** | **sanno** |

- `Devo prendere l'autobus?` — Do I need to take the bus?
- `Lei sa dov'è la posta?` — Do you know where the post office is?
- `Non so come arrivare.` — I don't know how to get there.

**Memory:** **devo = duty** (only a mnemonic, not an etymology); **so = I know** (learn this irregular form separately).

## 5. Directions: picture the route as arrows

| Visual | Italian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ⬆️ | **dritto** | Vada sempre dritto. |
| ↪️ | **a destra** | Giri a destra. |
| ↩️ | **a sinistra** | Giri a sinistra. |
| 🚥 | **al semaforo** | Giri al semaforo. |
| ➕ | **all'incrocio** | Arrivi all'incrocio. |
| 📍 | **alla fermata** | Scenda alla terza fermata. |

`Vada`, `giri`, `scenda` are **polite `Lei` directive forms** used in the textbook-style situation; their full imperative system is not the focus of the p. 85 summary. For familiar friends you can say `vai / gira / scendi`.

### Prepositions before places

- `davanti **alla** stazione` — in front of the station
- `di fronte **al** supermercato` — opposite the supermarket
- `accanto **alla** banca` — next to the bank
- `all'angolo` — on the corner
- `dietro **la** stazione` — behind the station (no `a` after `dietro` here)

Don't mechanically translate English “at/in/on” word by word. Memorize a **whole location phrase**.

## 6. `molto` as adverb or adjective

| Sentence | Function |
|---|---|
| `La città è **molto** vivace.` | adverb, **unchanged**, modifies adjective |
| `Ci sono **molti** musei.` | adjective agreeing with masculine plural |
| `Ci sono **molte** piazze.` | adjective agreeing with feminine plural |
| `C'è **molta** gente.` | adjective agreeing with feminine singular |

English usually has *very* versus *many/much*. Italian shares the stem, but changes the form **when it agrees with a noun**.

## 7. Questions: `dov'è …?`, `c'è …?`, `quale …?`

- `Dov'è la stazione?` asks **where a known place is**.
- `C'è una farmacia qui vicino?` asks **whether one exists nearby**.
- `Quale autobus devo prendere?` asks **which bus**.
- `A quale fermata devo scendere?` asks **at which stop**.

**Memory:** `c'è` → **exists?**; `dov'è` → **where exactly?**.

## 8. Time and opening hours (p. 84)

- `A che ora **parte** l'autobus?` — At what time does the bus leave?
- `Parte **all'una e mezza**.` — It leaves at 1:30.
- `A che ora **apre** il museo?` — At what time does the museum open?
- `**Alle nove**. Chiude **alle sei**.` — At nine. It closes at six.

**English bridge:** Italian generally doesn't insert a separate English-style auxiliary **does** in the question. `A che ora apre?` is the structure.

## Six-minute retrieval challenge

1. Say `un palazzo antico` in the plural with a partitive.
2. Convert `una chiesa famosa` to plural with a partitive.
3. Say `we must leave` using `dovere`.
4. Reply to `Vai a Padova?` using `ci`.
5. Explain `C'è un teatro?` versus `Dov'è il teatro?`.
